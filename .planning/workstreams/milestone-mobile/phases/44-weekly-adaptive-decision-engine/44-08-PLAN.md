  1. Sequential double fire: call `runWeeklyReview(userId, 'app_open_fallback')` and await it, then call `runWeeklyReview(userId, 'weekly_review_cron')` and await it. Assert exactly one `athlete_decisions` row with `decision_type = 'weekly_focus'` for that athlete, and that the second call returned `ran: false` with reason `already_recorded` — the cheap pre-check fired, so no second Claude call was paid for.
  2. Concurrent double fire (the real at-least-once scenario): invoke both sources with `Promise.all`, without awaiting the first. Assert exactly one `weekly_focus` row survives and that its `source` is one of the two valid values. At most one of the two results has `ran: true` with `success: true`; the other is either `already_recorded` (the pre-check won the race) or `ran: true, success: false, reason: 'duplicate'` (the RPC arbiter won).   3. `week_of` stability: assert the single surviving row's `week_of` equals the `YYYY-MM-DD` prefix of the athlete's `next_review_due_at` as it was before the fire, not today's date. This is the correctness property that makes cases 1 and 2 hold at all; if `week_of` were derived from the wall clock, two triggers on different days would both insert.
  4. Cost attribution (ENGINE-05): assert exactly one `ai_cost_log` row was written for that athlete, that its `source` is `'app_open_fallback'` or `'weekly_review_cron'` and is NOT `'user_chat'`, and that its `input_tokens` matches the scripted usage rather than zero.
  5. State patch scope (ENGINE-03): assert `athlete_state.readiness` changed to the scripted `new_readiness`, and that `level`, `points` and `tier` are unchanged from their post-onboarding values.
